# Short-Form Ranking Signals and Format Rules (TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels), 2025-2026, for Podcast/Stream Clips

> Method note: research date 2026-10-02. WebFetch was blocked by the egress proxy for every domain tried (newsroom.tiktok.com, tiktok.com, blog.youtube, support.google.com, buffer.com, opus.pro, socialmediatoday.com, techcrunch.com, engadget.com, tubefilter.com, ppc.land, searchenginejournal.com). All findings below come from web-search result summaries and snippets, not full-page reads. Numbers are reported as the search results attributed them. Sources are tagged **[OFFICIAL]** (platform docs or named platform staff), **[STUDY]** (third-party dataset with a stated sample), **[SECONDARY]** (reporting or aggregator), or **[FOLKLORE]** (unsourced creator or SEO-blog claims). Any finding dated before 2024 is flagged **(pre-2024, may be outdated)**.

## Q1. Which ranking signals does each platform officially say it uses, and how are completion, average percentage viewed, rewatches, shares/sends, saves, comments and follows weighted?

### Takeaway
All three platforms officially name viewer watch behavior as the main signal. TikTok says finishing a longer video is a "strong indicator". YouTube exposes "viewed vs. swiped away" as the Shorts feed decision metric. Instagram's Mosseri names watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach as the top three. No platform publishes numeric weights. Every "3-5x" or "most important signal" ranking you see online is a third-party estimate.

### Cited Findings
**TikTok (official)**
- [OFFICIAL] The For You feed ranks videos on a combination of factors. It starts from interests a new user expresses and adjusts for things the user marks as not interesting. Inputs include user interactions (videos liked or shared, accounts followed, comments posted, content created), video information and device/account settings. — [TikTok Newsroom, "How TikTok recommends videos #ForYou"](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you); [TikTok Safety Center, "Introduction to the TikTok recommendation system"](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/recommendation-system)
- [OFFICIAL] Weighting language: "a strong indicator of interest, such as whether a user finishes watching a longer video from beginning to end, would receive greater weight than a weak indicator, such as whether the video's viewer and creator are both in the same country." Neither follower count nor a past high-performing video is a direct ranking factor. — quoted via [Agorapulse](https://www.agorapulse.com/blog/tiktok/tiktok-algorithm/) and [Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/tiktok-algorithm/), from the Newsroom post **(the original Newsroom post is from 2020, so it is pre-2024 and may be outdated, but TikTok still links it)**
- [SECONDARY/FOLKLORE] Third-party rankings place "watch time relative to video length (% watched)" first and "replay rate" second. Others list "watch-time per impression, comment depth, share-to-DM ratio, re-watch loops, transcript relevance". These orderings are not from TikTok. — [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/tiktok-algorithm); [Voqus](https://www.voqusa.com/en/blog/tiktok-algorithm-2026)
- [SECONDARY] Since mid-2025, TikTok's auto-generated transcript reportedly feeds search indexing and For You relevance scoring. This matters for spoken podcast clips. — [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/tiktok-algorithm)
- [FOLKLORE] Claim that TikTok checks retention at 3-, 10- and 20-second checkpoints for longer videos. TikTok has not confirmed this. — [Benly / search summary](https://benly.ai/learn/tiktok-ads/creator-rewards-program-2026)

**YouTube Shorts (official plus staff statements)**
- [OFFICIAL] "Viewed vs. Swiped away" is a Shorts Analytics metric: the share of feed presentations where the viewer stayed to watch instead of swiping past. YouTube has not published how many seconds separates "viewed" from "swiped away". — [Prepublish.ai explainer](https://prepublish.ai/blog/viewed-vs-swiped-away-youtube-shorts); [Creator Essentials glossary](https://www.creatoressentials.com/glossary/viewed-vs-swiped-away/)
- [OFFICIAL, via secondary] Shorts product lead Todd Sherman explained on Creator Insider why this metric matters. Shorts distribution is described as explore/exploit: a Short goes first to a small seed audience and expands if that audience engages. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- [OFFICIAL, via secondary] YouTube staff (Todd Sherman, Rene Ritchie) say Shorts and long-form run on separate recommendation systems, and that Shorts do not hurt a channel's long-form recommendations. YouTube's internal comparison found channels posting both formats grew faster than long-form-only channels. — [Creaticalc](https://creaticalc.com/blog/do-youtube-shorts-hurt-long-form); [Panda Video](https://www.pandavideo.com/blog/shorts-and-long-form-videos-same-channel)
- [FOLKLORE] Shorts are said to lean on the swipe-vs-watch ratio in the first 1-3 s, replay rate and shares, with metadata weighted less. — [search summary of vidIQ/Shortimize-type guides](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/how-does-youtube-shorts-algorithm-work)

**Instagram Reels (official)**
- [OFFICIAL] In January 2025, Adam Mosseri named the three most important ranking signals across surfaces: **watch time, likes per reach, sends per reach**. Quote: "Likes are slightly more important for connected content, and sends are slightly more important for unconnected content." — [Kompozy news summary](https://kompozy.io/news/instagram-mosseri-ranking-signals-guidance); [Blck Alpaca](https://blckalpaca.at/en/knowledge-base/social-media/social-media-algorithms-distribution/instagram-algorithm-2026); [Dataslayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/instagram-algorithm-2025-complete-guide-for-marketers)
- [CONFLICT/FOLKLORE] The claim that sends carry "3-5x the weight of likes" for non-follower distribution is attributed to Mosseri by some blogs, e.g. [Socialync](https://www.socialync.io/blog/adam-mosseri-shares-instagram-algorithm-2026) and [eClincher](https://www.eclincher.com/articles/how-the-instagram-algorithm-works-in-2026). [Dataslayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/instagram-algorithm-2025-complete-guide-for-marketers) says it is a community-derived estimate and that Meta has not confirmed an exact ratio. Treat it as unconfirmed.
- [FOLKLORE] Claims that Instagram "replaced 3-second view count with total watch time plus replay rate", and that "a 15-s Reel watched 3x outranks a 60-s Reel watched once". These are illustrative, not official. — [Dataslayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/instagram-algorithm-2025-complete-guide-for-marketers)

**Saves, comments, follows**
- No source found this session gives official relative weights for saves, comments or follows on any of the three platforms. TikTok lists comments and follows as interaction inputs without weights ([TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you)). Socialinsider reports 2025 per-video comment averages of TikTok 50, Reels 20, Shorts 10 ([Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/tiktok-vs-reels-vs-shorts/)).

### Inferences
- A cross-platform clip score should weight, from highest to lowest: (1) predicted first-seconds hold (anti-swipe); (2) predicted completion and percentage viewed relative to length; (3) predicted "sendability" or shareability (strongest for non-follower reach on Instagram, and a named interaction on TikTok); (4) loop/rewatch potential; (5) comment-provoking content; (6) save value. Items 1-2 are officially the core on all three platforms. Items 3-6 have official mentions but no published weights, so they should be tunable hyperparameters, not fixed constants.
- Because TikTok's transcript reportedly feeds relevance, clips should have clean speech and an on-topic keyword in the first sentence. Burned-in captions help the viewer, but the spoken audio matters for indexing.

### Gaps
- No platform publishes numeric signal weights. All "X times more important" numbers are unverified.
- Full text of TikTok's Newsroom post and YouTube Help could not be fetched to verify exact current wording (egress blocked).
- I found no official statement on how saves are weighted relative to sends or likes on Reels in 2025-2026.

## Q2. What changed in 2025-2026 (view counting, maximum lengths, Trial Reels, originality penalties)?

### Takeaway
Every platform now allows clips up to 3 minutes or longer. Each still rewards strong retention, and each has tightened originality enforcement against re-uploaded or aggregated clips. The newest change is YouTube's 1 Oct 2026 announcement that Shorts recommendations will cut reach for barely-changed re-uploads. For a clipping tool, the highest-impact rule is YouTube's: a Short over 60 s with any active Content ID claim is blocked globally.

### Cited Findings
**YouTube Shorts**
- [OFFICIAL] Shorts maximum length rose to **3 minutes (180 s)** for uploads from **15 Oct 2024**, for square or taller aspect ratios. YouTube said it would improve recommendations for longer Shorts "in the coming months" (Todd Sherman). — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2024/10/03/youtube-shorts-3-minutes/); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-extends-shorts-to-3-minutes-adds-new-features/529177/); [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/tall-updates-coming-to-shorts/)
- [OFFICIAL] **Any Short over 1 minute with an active Content ID claim of any type, including manual claims, is blocked globally.** It is not playable, not recommended and not monetizable. There is no channel penalty, and nothing changes for Shorts under 1 minute. — [YouTube Help, "Understand three-minute YouTube Shorts"](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en); [TeamYouTube on X](https://x.com/TeamYouTube/status/2000198352625910075)
- [OFFICIAL] From **31 Mar 2025**, a Shorts view counts each time a Short starts or replays, with no minimum watch time. The older threshold-based metric survives as **"engaged views"**, which YouTube uses for YPP eligibility and Shorts revenue sharing. Public view counts after that date are inflated relative to older ones. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-updates-shorts-views-watch-time/743629/); [gHacks](https://www.ghacks.net/2025/03/27/youtube-updates-shorts-view-count-methodology-to-align-with-industry-standards/)
- [OFFICIAL] On **15 Jul 2025**, YouTube renamed its "repetitious content" monetization policy to **"inauthentic content"**, covering mass-produced or repetitive content including templated AI content. The **reused content policy (commentary, clips, compilations, reactions) was unchanged**: clips are allowed if they add significant original value. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/); [YouTube Help, channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- [OFFICIAL] On **1 Oct 2026**, Creator Liaison Rene Ritchie (Creator Insider) said **Shorts recommendations will favour original content and reduce reach for channels re-uploading barely-changed clips**. Remixers must add their own value, "not just VO descriptions of what's happening on screen, minor technical edits, or template-based bulk changes". The change affects recommendations only (monetization stays under the reused-content policy). YouTube gave no start date and offers no dashboard showing whether a channel is affected. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-original-shorts-reposted-clips-reach/591774/); [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/youtube/youtube-shorts-original-content-reach-update/)

**TikTok**
- [OFFICIAL] Unoriginal content is ineligible for the For You feed (FYF). That covers content copied completely from others, content largely repurposed without creative edits, and content combined from multiple sources with little added value. Also ineligible: content with a visible watermark, logo or QR code, and "low quality" content such as "extremely short clips". Content "imported or uploaded from TikTok, other platforms, or other sources including television, movies, or **webcasts**" without new creative edits counts as unoriginal. The penalty is FYF suppression, not removal. — [TikTok Creator Academy, originality policy](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/article/tiktok-originality-policy); [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/tiktok-ban-you-page-change-guidelines-viral-app-1680214) **(Newsweek piece is 2022, pre-2024; the policy persists in current Creator Academy guidance)**
- [SECONDARY] Creator Rewards Program pays only on **original videos longer than 1 minute**, reportedly about $0.50-$2.00+ per 1,000 qualified views. This is commonly cited as the reason TikTok's ecosystem shifted toward 1-minute-plus videos. — [Benly](https://benly.ai/learn/tiktok-ads/creator-rewards-program-2026); [Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/how-much-does-tiktok-pay-per-view). (Benly says it "replaced the Creator Fund in 2025". Other reporting dates the transition to 2023-2024, so the date is uncertain.)

**Instagram**
- [OFFICIAL] Reels maximum length went from 90 s to **3 minutes in January 2025** (announced by Mosseri). — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/instagram-reels/); [Vidico](https://vidico.com/news/instagram-reels-statistics/)
- [CONFLICT] [Creatorflow / search summary](https://creatorflow.so/blog/instagram-algorithm-2026/) says Instagram still pushes Reels under 90 s to non-followers, so 90-180 s Reels get less unconnected reach. I found no 2025-2026 official confirmation; this may be carried over from the pre-2025 rule that Reels over 90 s were not recommended.
- [OFFICIAL] **Trial Reels**: launched late 2024 and expanded through 2025 (reportedly to creators with 1,000+ followers). A Trial Reel is shown only to non-followers. The creator can then share it to followers if it performs (third parties say after a 24-72 h window). Scheduling of Trial Reels was added later. — [Social Media Today, expansion](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-expands-access-trial-reels/752895/); [Social Media Today, scheduling](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-allows-creators-to-schedule-trial-reels/816549/); [Rapzilla](https://rapzilla.com/2025-02-instagram-release-reels-feature-creators-experiment/)
- [OFFICIAL] **Originality and aggregators**: Instagram swaps reposted content for the original in recommendations. Accounts that repost others' content without significant edits **10+ times in 30 days** lose recommendation eligibility. Mosseri: aggregators must meaningfully transform posts, and **changing the speed of a video or adding a screenshot of the original poster's name is not enough**. Voice-overs, reaction clips and material meme edits are treated as transformative. The aggregator policy first applied to Reels; a later update (reported December 2025 / 2026) extended it to photos and carousels. Penalized accounts lose Explore, the Reels tab, suggested posts and Search. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/instagrams-algorithm-overhaul-will-reward-original-content-and-penalize-aggregators-130018977.html) **(original announcement April 2024)**; [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-updates-algorithm-to-benefit-original-creators/819016/); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/04/30/instagram-removes-algorithm-recommendations-repost-content-aggregator/)
- [FOLKLORE] Repost-heavy accounts reportedly saw 60-80% reach drops after the December 2025 update, and original content reportedly gets 40-60% more distribution than reposts. Neither figure has an official source. — [Hookstudio](https://www.hookstudio.ai/blog/2025-instagram-repost-penalty-aggregator-accounts-crushed); [Growvib](https://growvib.com/blog/instagram-aggregator-crackdown-repost-accounts-2026); [Dataslayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/instagram-algorithm-2025-complete-guide-for-marketers)
- [SECONDARY] Visible watermarks from other platforms (especially TikTok) suppress Reels reach. Instagram originally stated this in its 2021 Reels guidance **(pre-2024)**, and secondary sources say it still applies. — [Dataslayer / search summary](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/instagram-algorithm-2025-complete-guide-for-marketers)
- [OFFICIAL] Instagram added **Skip rate** to Reels Insights, replacing "View rate" (reported August 2025). Meta exposed skip rate in the Marketing API Insights endpoint in December 2025. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-adds-retention-insights-reels/758464/); [Babbleboxx](https://www.babbleboxx.com/post/instagram-adds-reels-retention-skip-rate-what-influencer-marketers-should-do-next); [Storrito](https://storrito.com/resources/how-instagrams-updated-marketing-api-metrics-work/)

### Inferences
- **Hard export rule for YouTube:** if a clip contains any third-party claimable material (music beds, TV/film inserts, or another creator's podcast/stream enrolled in Content ID), cap the YouTube Shorts export at **≤ 59 s**. Allow 60-180 s only for fully owned audio and video.
- **Watermark rule:** always render clean masters per platform. Never cross-post a file carrying another platform's watermark, overlay, logo or QR code (it is FYF-ineligible on TikTok and suppressed on Instagram).
- **Transformation rule for third-party clipping** (clip farms clipping someone else's podcast or stream): captions or re-crops alone are probably insufficient on all three platforms. The tool should support added commentary, a voice-over, a reaction cam, or narrative recontextualization (setup card, context, payoff). YouTube's 1 Oct 2026 wording explicitly excludes "template-based bulk changes", which describes many auto-clipping pipelines.
- Analytics ingestion should use YouTube "engaged views", not public views, for any 2025+ performance modeling. Public views after 31 Mar 2025 are not comparable with earlier data.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether Instagram still limits non-follower recommendation of 90-180 s Reels in 2025-2026.
- I found no official enforcement date or measurable threshold for YouTube's 1 Oct 2026 Shorts originality change.
- I did not verify TikTok's 2025-2026 upload limits (reports of 10-minute and 60-minute uploads) or any change to the For You algorithm after the 2026 US joint-venture restructuring (no reliable source found this session).

## Q3. Which clip lengths perform best on each platform per data studies, and what are the retention/completion benchmarks?

### Takeaway
Reach and views rise with length past about 60 s on TikTok (Buffer, Socialinsider). Engagement rate is highest under 30 s, dips at 30-60 s, and recovers past 3 minutes. YouTube Shorts studies conflict: mean-view studies favour 50-60 s, while a median-view study favours 11-20 s. Podcast clips cluster at 30-60 s. Completion "benchmarks" mostly come from low-rigour SEO sources and should be treated only as priors.

### Cited Findings
**TikTok**
- [STUDY] Buffer, 1.1M TikToks (published March 2025): ~86% of TikToks are under 1 minute. Videos over 60 s got **+43.2% reach vs 30-60 s, +70.3% vs 10-30 s, +95.7% vs 5-10 s**, and **+63.8% watch time**. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/longer-tiktoks-get-more-views-data/); [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/research-tiktok-longer-videos-get-more-reach/743067/)
- [STUDY] Socialinsider TikTok length analysis: engagement rate is **6.00% for under 30 s, 4.20% for 30-60 s, 5.80% for over 180 s** (U-shape). Views rise with length almost across the board, with the biggest gains past 1 minute. Their summary: "30 seconds for engagement, 2 minutes for views." — [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/how-long-are-tiktok-videos/)
- [SECONDARY] Analyses of millions of TikToks reportedly show 3-10 minute videos getting roughly double the views of 6-10 s videos. — [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/short-form-video-statistics-tiktok-reels-and-shorts-by-the-numbers-in-2026/) (search summary; underlying dataset not verified)

**YouTube Shorts**
- [STUDY] Inflow Network, 5,400 Shorts (likely the pre-3-minute era): average views by length were <10 s 77K, 10-20 s 90K, 20-30 s 461K, 30-40 s 690K, 40-50 s 800K, **50-60 s 1.7M** (about 22x the under-10 s bracket). These are means, which outliers skew. — [Piktochart / search summary](https://piktochart.com/blog/how-long-youtube-shorts/)
- [STUDY, CONFLICTING] quso.ai, 108K+ "matured" Shorts: the **11-20 s band has the highest median views** (median 901). — [quso.ai](https://quso.ai/research/youtube-shorts-length). (The conflict with Inflow is probably mean vs median and small vs large channels.)
- [SURVEY] Adobe Express creator survey: 46-53% of creators say under 30 s is best for views. This is opinion, not performance data. — [Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/youtube-shorts-length-study)
- [STUDY] Opus Clip, **494,716 YouTube podcast clips**: the most common length is **30-60 s (44.7% of clips)**. Across 2.88M YouTube clips, 40.1% fall in 30-60 s, and Podcast Clips is the largest category (10.7%). This is the posting distribution of Opus users, not proof of best performance. — [Opus Clip research, podcast clips](https://www.opus.pro/research/best-time-podcast-clips-youtube); [Opus Clip research, YouTube guide](https://www.opus.pro/research/youtube-video-guide)

**Instagram Reels**
- [SECONDARY] Reels of 15-30 s reportedly average 5.8% engagement, and an overall "sweet spot" of 21-34 s is claimed. — [Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/video-length-sweet-spots-tiktok-reels-shorts) (dataset not verified)
- [STUDY] Socialinsider 2025 engagement-rate benchmarks: **TikTok 3.70%** (up from 2.50%), **Reels 0.48%**, **Shorts 0.27%**. TikTok averages 248 shares per post (+45%). Sample: 69M videos, Jan 2025-Jul 2026. Engagement-rate definitions differ by platform, so cross-platform comparison is approximate. — [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/tiktok-vs-reels-vs-shorts/); [The Keyword](https://www.thekeyword.co/news/tiktok-engagement-rate-benchmark-2026)

**Retention / completion benchmarks (low confidence)**
- [FOLKLORE/SECONDARY] TikTok completion targets: **<15 s → 80%+**, **15-30 s → 60%+**, **30 s+ → 50%+**. Reels around 15 s → 60-70% to the end. Shorts 15-30 s "strong" completion threshold moved from 35% to 40%. Videos under 90 s retain about 50% on average. "A 45-s video at 70% completion beats a 15-s video at 40%." — search summary drawing on [Retensis](https://retensis.com/blog/audience-retention-benchmarks-2026), [Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/compare-watch-time-tiktok-vs-reels-vs-shorts) and [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/short-form-video-statistics-tiktok-reels-and-shorts-by-the-numbers-in-2026/). Which number comes from which source is uncertain, and none publishes its methodology.
- [STUDY-ish] About 70%+ of Shorts exceed 15 s, few use the full 60 s, and the average is about 20-40 s. — [search summary, Kapwing/Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/video-length-sweet-spots-tiktok-reels-shorts)
- [SECONDARY] Opus Clip guidance: 30-90 s typically performs best for podcast clips across the three platforms. — [Opus Clip blog](https://www.opus.pro/blog/how-to-edit-podcast-shorts-like-a-pro-using-opus-clip-pc-mac) (vendor guidance)

### Inferences
- Suggested export-length presets (inferred, for tuning):
  - **TikTok:** two modes. "Punch" 20-30 s for maximum engagement rate and loopability. "Story" 61-120 s+ for reach, watch time and Creator Rewards eligibility (owned/original accounts only). Avoid landing clips at 30-60 s by default unless retention is predicted high.
  - **YouTube Shorts:** default **30-59 s**. Cap at 59 s when any claimable third-party content is present. Use 60-180 s only for owned content with a strong narrative arc.
  - **Instagram Reels:** default **15-60 s**, 90 s soft maximum for non-follower reach given the unresolved 90-s question. Use 90-180 s only for follower-facing or Trial Reels tests.
- The scoring model should optimize **expected watch seconds × completion probability**, not length alone. Studies favouring longer clips reflect survivors that held attention. Padding a weak moment to 61 s will lower completion and likely hurt.
- Treat completion benchmarks as length-conditional priors, roughly: under 15 s ≥ 70-80%; 15-30 s ≥ 60%; 30-60 s ≥ 45-50%; over 60 s ≥ 35-40%. Calibrate against the user's own analytics.

### Gaps
- No Buffer, Hootsuite, Sprout, Metricool, Emplifi or Dash Hudson dataset found this session gives **completion rate or average percentage viewed by length bucket** with a stated sample. The available benchmarks are unsourced.
- I found no large 2025-2026 study on 60-180 s Shorts performance after the 3-minute extension.
- No Reels length-vs-reach study with a disclosed large sample was found. The Reels length numbers above are from unverified blogs.

## Q4. What is the role of the first 1-3 seconds, and are there published benchmarks for "viewed vs swiped away" or 3-second hold?

### Takeaway
Two platforms now expose a first-seconds metric directly: YouTube Shorts "viewed vs. swiped away" (threshold unpublished) and Instagram Reels "skip rate" (scrolled past within 3 s, added around August 2025). Third-party benchmarks converge: about 70-90% viewed on Shorts is good and under about 60% is poor, while a Reels skip rate under about 30-40% is healthy and over about 50% means the hook failed. No official benchmark exists on any platform.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] YouTube Shorts "Viewed vs. Swiped away" measures the share of feed impressions where viewers stayed instead of swiping. The cutoff in seconds is not published. — [Prepublish.ai](https://prepublish.ai/blog/viewed-vs-swiped-away-youtube-shorts); [Socialinsider Shorts analytics guide](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics-guide/)
- [STUDY] A study of 3.3 billion Shorts views: Shorts at **70-90% viewed** did better, and those **under 60%** performed poorly. — [Medium, "Cracking the YouTube Shorts Algorithm: A Study of 3.3 Billion Views"](https://medium.com/@antoinelacombled/cracking-the-youtube-shorts-algorithm-a-study-of-3-3-billion-views-4711fdf7931b)
- [FOLKLORE] Swipe-away under 30% "expands distribution", and over 50% "effectively kills the video". — [search summary of vidIQ/Shortimize-type guides](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- [OFFICIAL] Instagram **Skip rate** is the percentage of viewers who scroll past a Reel **within the first 3 seconds**. It sits in the Retention card of Reel Insights, was added around August 2025 replacing "View rate", and has been in the API since December 2025. — [Babbleboxx](https://www.babbleboxx.com/post/instagram-adds-reels-retention-skip-rate-what-influencer-marketers-should-do-next); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-adds-retention-insights-reels/758464/); [Storrito](https://storrito.com/resources/how-instagrams-updated-marketing-api-metrics-work/)
- [FOLKLORE] Reels skip rate under about 30-40% is healthy and over about 50% signals a failing hook. — [Babbleboxx / industry trackers](https://www.babbleboxx.com/post/instagram-adds-reels-retention-skip-rate-what-influencer-marketers-should-do-next)
- [OFFICIAL, via secondary] YouTube Shorts uses explore/exploit: the seed-audience response (dominated by early swipe behavior) gates wider distribution. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)

### Inferences
- The hook score (first 1-3 s) should be the single largest weight in clip selection and in trimming the start point. Practical implications for a clipper:
  - Start on the line that creates the question or tension. Cut lead-in breaths and "so, um".
  - Put an on-screen text hook in frame 1.
  - Avoid black or intro frames and logos at t=0.
  - Prefer an in-motion or mid-sentence start over a cold open of silence.
- Proposed target KPIs for the tool's feedback loop: Shorts viewed ≥ 70% (alert under 60%); Reels skip rate ≤ 35% (alert over 50%). TikTok exposes no equivalent public metric in the sources found, so use 2-3 s retention from the TikTok retention curve as a proxy.

### Gaps
- No platform publishes official benchmarks for viewed-vs-swiped or skip rate.
- The exact YouTube "viewed" threshold (in seconds) is unpublished.
- I found no TikTok-specific published 3-second hold benchmark with a disclosed sample.

## Q5. What does the data say about posting cadence and timing, and does posting many clips from one episode help or hurt? What about duplicate/near-duplicate penalties?

### Takeaway
Buffer's large studies show per-post reach rising with frequency, with diminishing returns: TikTok gains most moving from 1 to 2-5 posts per week and keeps rising to 11+; Instagram rises up to 10+ posts per week. Posting many clips from one episode is not officially penalized. Near-duplicates are the risk: YouTube's "inauthentic/repetitive" policy and its Oct 2026 Shorts change, TikTok's unoriginal-content FYF rule, and Instagram's 10-reposts-in-30-days rule for third-party content.

### Cited Findings
- [STUDY] Buffer, **11.4M TikTok posts from 150K+ accounts**: compared with 1 post per week, views per post were **+17% at 2-5/week, +29% at 6-10/week, +34% at 11+/week**. The steepest gain is the first step, then diminishing returns. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/study-shows-2-5-weekly-tiktoks-deliver-biggest-view-increase/558641/); [Wavevision summary](https://www.wavevision.io/blog/tiktok-posting-frequency-vs-growth-case-study-what-11-4-million-posts-reveal)
- [STUDY] Buffer Instagram frequency: compared with 1-2 posts per week, reach per post was **+12% at 3-5/week, +18% at 6-9/week, +24% at 10+/week**. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/study-shows-posting-more-instagram-leads-to-more-reach/757633/); [Buffer frequency guide](https://buffer.com/resources/social-media-frequency-guide/)
- [STUDY] Buffer consistency study: accounts posting in at least 20 of 26 weeks earned **450% more engagement per post** than sporadic posters. — [Buffer creator growth playbook / search summary](https://buffer.com/resources/creator-growth-playbook/)
- [STUDY] Sprout Social 2025 (about 30K brands and creators): Instagram best times are **Tue 1-7 pm and Wed 12-9 pm local**. TikTok best times are **Tue-Thu 2-6 pm local** (Mon 3-5, Tue 2-6, Wed 1-8, Thu 1-5). Weekends are generally weaker, except TikTok stays reasonable. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/best-times-to-post-2025-sprout-social/744014/); [Sprout Social TikTok](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-tiktok/) (brand-account skew; may not transfer to clip accounts)
- [STUDY] Opus Clip: posting of podcast clips on YouTube peaks **4-7 pm UTC (11 am-2 pm EST)**, and **Tuesday** is the most active day. This describes when Opus users post, not proven performance. — [Opus Clip research](https://www.opus.pro/research/best-time-podcast-clips-youtube)
- [OFFICIAL] YouTube's inauthentic-content policy (July 2025) targets "similar or repetitive content" and templated mass production for monetization. The 1 Oct 2026 Shorts change cuts recommendation reach for barely-changed re-uploads and "template-based bulk changes". — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-original-shorts-reposted-clips-reach/591774/)
- [OFFICIAL, via secondary] YouTube staff say Shorts do not cannibalize long-form. Secondary sources warn that frequent Shorts hurt when they "repeat the same idea with different wording" or recycled hooks. — [Creaticalc](https://creaticalc.com/blog/do-youtube-shorts-hurt-long-form); [Circleboom](https://circleboom.com/blog/should-you-post-multiple-youtube-shorts-a-day/)
- [SECONDARY] Podcast clip practice: 3-5 clips per episode, with some guides recommending 5-10 released over 1-2 weeks, plus a call to action to the full episode (e.g., about 3 clips per episode on Ali Abdaal's podcast). — [Fame](https://www.fame.so/post/ultimate-podcast-clip-guide); [Choppity](https://www.choppity.com/blog/how-to-repurpose-podcast-into-shorts/); [YouTube Blog podcast guide](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/the-definitive-guide-to-creating-engaging-podcast-content/)
- [FOLKLORE] "Three or four high-quality Reels per week outperform daily filler." This is unverified opinion. — [search summary](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/instagram-algorithm-2025-complete-guide-for-marketers)

### Inferences
- Default cadence presets: **TikTok 1-2 per day** (the gain flattens beyond about 11/week). **Instagram 1 per day** (6-10/week). **Shorts 1-3 per day**. Space clips from the same episode at least a few hours apart, and spread one episode's clips over 5-14 days instead of dumping them at once.
- **Near-duplicate guard:** the tool should de-duplicate on transcript overlap. Do not export two clips sharing more than about 30-40% of the same spoken content, and do not re-export the same moment with only a new hook, caption or crop on the same account. This mitigates YouTube "repetitive/template" and TikTok "largely repurposed" risk. The threshold is inferred and should be tuned.
- Many distinct moments per episode are fine (and favoured by the frequency data). The risk is template-identical packaging at scale, so vary caption styles, hooks and structure.

### Gaps
- I found no platform statement or study quantifying reach impact from posting N clips from the same source episode.
- No published threshold exists for when two clips count as "near-duplicates" on any platform.
- I found no YouTube Shorts-specific posting-frequency study with a disclosed sample (Buffer's covered TikTok and Instagram).

## Q6. What specifically applies to podcast and livestream clip channels (clipping farms, Twitch/Kick streamer clips, podcast clip accounts), and how do they grow?

### Takeaway
A paid "clip economy" now drives much streamer and podcast reach. Creators and marketplaces pay clippers per 1,000 views: Whop averages about $1, Vyro (MrBeast/ViewStats) a flat $3, and Kick gambling-stream campaigns $10-30. Platforms are simultaneously tightening originality rules aimed at exactly this kind of mass re-uploading. Owned-channel podcast clips sit on safe ground. Third-party clip farms need transformation and must avoid long claimed Shorts.

### Cited Findings
- [SECONDARY] Clippers are paid per 1,000 verified views. **Whop Content Rewards** pays **$0.20-$6 per 1,000 views, averaging about $1**. **Vyro**, launched October 2025 by MrBeast and ViewStats, pays a **flat $3 per 1,000 views, capped at $1,000 per clip**, with hourly payouts. **Kick** gambling-stream campaigns pay **$10+ per 1,000**, and NPR documented rates up to $25. — [OpenClip guides](https://openclip.app/guides/get-paid-to-clip-streamers); [OpenClip, Kick](https://openclip.app/guides/clipping-on-kick); [OpenClip, earnings](https://openclip.app/guides/how-much-do-clippers-make) (vendor sources)
- [SECONDARY] Streamer N3on reportedly paid **303 people $1.4M over five weeks** to clip his streams into shorts. Streamer Clavicular said his clippers get **$30 per 1,000 views** (Kick-backed). — [CreatorDB, "The Clip Economy"](https://creatordb.app/creator-news/the-rise-of-the-clip-economy/); [Business of TV (Substack)](https://businessoftv.substack.com/p/clippers-and-view-botting-big-social)
- [SECONDARY] Business of TV links the clipping trend to view-botting concerns, a reason for platforms to tighten originality and view-validity rules. — [Business of TV](https://businessoftv.substack.com/p/clippers-and-view-botting-big-social)
- [STUDY] Podcast clips are the **largest content category** among Opus Clip YouTube outputs (10.7%), with **44.7% at 30-60 s**. — [Opus Clip research](https://www.opus.pro/research/best-time-podcast-clips-youtube)
- [OFFICIAL] TikTok explicitly lists **webcasts** among sources whose uploads count as unoriginal without new creative edits (directly relevant to livestream clips). Watermarked or logo-bearing clips are FYF-ineligible. — [TikTok Creator Academy](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/article/tiktok-originality-policy); [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/tiktok-ban-you-page-change-guidelines-viral-app-1680214) (2022)
- [OFFICIAL] YouTube's reused-content policy allows clips and compilations with significant original value. The 1 Oct 2026 Shorts change asks remixers for "commentary, analysis, and storytelling", and says voice-overs that merely describe the screen do not count. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/); [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/youtube/youtube-shorts-original-content-reach-update/)
- [OFFICIAL] Instagram removes accounts from recommendations at 10+ unoriginal reposts in 30 days. Reaction clips and voice-overs are treated as transformative, while speed changes and credit screenshots are not. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/instagrams-algorithm-overhaul-will-reward-original-content-and-penalize-aggregators-130018977.html); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-updates-algorithm-to-benefit-original-creators/819016/)
- [SECONDARY] Podcast growth playbooks treat clips as top-of-funnel and always point back to the full episode in the caption or comments. One unverified claim says 5 exposures in a week make a viewer "12x more likely" to check out the full episode. — [Fame](https://www.fame.so/post/ultimate-podcast-clip-guide); [Klypse](https://www.klypse.app/blog/podcast-to-short-clips) (the 12x claim is folklore)

### Inferences
- The tool needs an **"ownership mode" flag** per source:
  - **Owned** (the user's own podcast/stream): originality-safe. Allow 60-180 s on YouTube if no third-party claims, and allow TikTok 61 s+ for Creator Rewards.
  - **Licensed / clipper-program** (e.g., paid campaigns where the streamer authorizes clipping): the rights holder may whitelist the channel for Content ID. Otherwise apply the ≤ 59 s Shorts cap.
  - **Unlicensed third-party:** require transformation layers (commentary voice-over, reaction cam, context cards). Cap the Instagram volume per account to stay well under 10 untransformed reposts per 30 days, and expect reduced YouTube Shorts reach after Oct 2026.
- Clip-farm growth historically relied on volume across many accounts. The 2025-2026 policy direction (Instagram 10/30 rule, YouTube inauthentic and Oct 2026 change, TikTok unoriginal FYF) makes pure volume with templated packaging increasingly risky. Scoring should reward per-clip uniqueness, not raw output count.
- For stream clips specifically, strip platform overlays (Twitch/Kick chat boxes, alerts, sponsor QR codes) or crop them out. QR codes and logos are explicit TikTok FYF-ineligibility triggers.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable data on growth curves (followers or views over time) for podcast clip accounts or streamer clip channels.
- I found no official statement from any platform on how authorized clipper-program content (e.g., Whop, Vyro, Kick campaigns) is treated for originality.
- Clip-economy payout figures come from vendor sites and secondary reporting, and could not be fetched in full to verify the NPR citation.

# Short index puts win 85%, but no one has confirmed the edge

Only one family of strategies available at Robinhood options Level 3 reliably shows win rates of 85% or more across thousands of trades: **selling out-of-the-money S&P 500 puts (SPY or SPX), either cash-secured or as bull put credit spreads, at roughly 16–30 delta and 30–60 days to expiration**. A 41,600-trade SPY study found 16-delta short puts won often enough that a 25% profit target produced a **98% success rate**. The same study shows why the win rate is the wrong target: that rule needed **95.8% wins just to break even**, because each loss was so much larger than each win ([projectfinance](https://www.projectfinance.com/short-put-management/)). No source in the research confirms net profitability after costs on 1,000 or more *out-of-sample* trades. The best independent evidence is also getting weaker. Equity index option alphas have been statistically indistinguishable from zero for about 15 years ([Chicago Fed, Dew-Becker & Giglio 2025](https://www.chicagofed.org/publications/working-papers/2025/2025-17)), and simple put-write and buy-write strategies did not beat the S&P 500 on real 2012–2023 options data ([Kumiega et al. 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7072/12/4/114)). Equity mean-reversion systems such as RSI(2), Double 7s and IBS top out at **74–83% wins** in backtests. Crypto dip-buying on Robinhood is roughly break-even or worse once its spread costs are included. The practical answer is therefore a short list of pre-specified rule sets to test, not a proven winner. Each one wins by selling crash insurance, so it needs a test protocol that can fail it. ## An 85% hit rate is cheap; positive expectancy is not

The target itself needs a correction before any strategy is judged. With win rate *p*, average win *W* and average loss *L*, expectancy per trade is *pW − (1−p)L*. The breakeven payoff ratio (*W/L*) is *(1−p)/p*. At 85% wins, the average loss can be up to **5.67× the average win** before costs and the strategy still breaks even. At 90% the limit is 9×; at 95% it is 19×. The consequence is that a high hit rate can be manufactured with zero edge. If a random-walk trade has a profit target of *a* and a stop of *b*, it hits the target first with probability of roughly *b/(a+b)*. So a stop placed 5.67× farther away than the target produces an 85% "win rate" on pure noise. Every strategy that reaches 85% does it this way: a close target with a distant stop, no stop at all, or a short option position. All of these create a **negatively skewed** profile. The Cboe PUT index shows the pattern. It had a higher Sharpe ratio than the S&P 500 but *more negative skewness* ([Neuberger Berman / Bondarenko](https://www.nb.com/documents/public/en-us/uncovering_the_equity_index_putwrite_strategy_ria.pdf)). The statistical tools used to confirm a track record penalize this shape. The minimum track record length rises as skew becomes more negative and kurtosis rises ([Bailey & López de Prado](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1821643)).

The size of the sample makes this concrete. At 1,000 trades, an observed 85% win rate has a 95% Wilson interval of about **82.7%–87.1%** ([Wilson interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binomial_proportion_confidence_interval)). To show that the true rate is at least 85%, you therefore need to observe about **87%** or more. Profitability is harder to confirm still. Take a strategy that wins +1% 85% of the time and loses −4.5% the rest of the time. Its expectancy is a healthy +0.175% per trade, yet its t-statistic over 1,000 trades is only about 2.8. That falls short of the **t > 3** hurdle recommended when many rules have been tested ([Harvey, Liu & Zhu, RFS 2016](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/29/1/5/1843824)). There is a further catch. Overlapping option trades opened daily on one index are not 1,000 independent draws, and neither are dip-buys across 500 stocks on the same selloff day. The *effective* sample can be closer to 150–250 observations, and at that size the confidence interval around 85% is roughly ±5 points.

## Short S&P 500 puts are the only candidates that clear 85% at scale

### The evidence base and its limits

The volatility risk premium explains why short puts win so often. Implied variance on stock indexes exceeds subsequent realized variance on average ([Carr & Wu 2009](https://engineering.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/2019-01/CarrReviewofFinStudiesMarch2009-a.pdf)). From 1990 to 2018, S&P 500 implied volatility averaged **19.3%** against **15.1%** realized ([Bondarenko 2019, Cboe](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/education/research_publications/PutWriteCBOE19_v14_by_Prof_Oleg_Bondarenko_as_of_June_14.pdf)). The benchmark PUT index sells at-the-money puts, so its win rate is far below 85%. Over 1986–2018 it compounded **9.54% a year versus 9.80%** for the S&P 500, with about two-thirds of the volatility (same source). Its maximum drawdown over 2006–2018 was **−32.7% versus −50.9%** for the S&P 500. Cboe commissioned these studies, and Cboe earns fees on SPX volume ([Cboe IR](https://ir.cboe.com/news/news-details/2016/New-Study-On-Weekly-Monthly-SP-500-PutWrite-Indices-Released-01-27-2016/default.aspx)). The independent evidence is less favorable. Kumiega et al. used real options data and found that no simple passive option strategy beat the S&P 500 over 2012–2023 ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7072/12/4/114)). Dew-Becker and Giglio attribute the disappearance of option alpha to falling trading frictions ([Chicago Fed](https://www.chicagofed.org/publications/working-papers/2025/2025-17)).

The 85% win rate comes from moving the short strike out of the money. A vendor summary of tastytrade research claims that 45-DTE, 16-delta SPY puts expired worthless about **95%** of the time over 2005–2020, and that 30-delta puts won about 89% ([ThetaLoop, secondary](https://thetaloop.app/learn/delta-guide)). The primary tastylive study was not located. The more useful independent studies agree on the direction. In projectfinance's 16-delta, 30–60 DTE SPY study (41,600 trades, 2007–2017), holding to expiration gave the **highest average P/L per trade** ([projectfinance](https://www.projectfinance.com/short-put-management/)). In Spintwig's 45-DTE SPY cash-secured put tests, hold-to-expiry had roughly the best Sharpe ratio, with 50% and 75% take-profits close behind ([7 Circles on Spintwig](https://the7circles.uk/options-11-spintwig-efficiency/)). For spreads, Spintwig ran more than 52,400 short SPX put-vertical trades at 45 DTE. Opening only when a regime filter was true, then holding to expiration, beat opening every day on return, drawdown and drawdown duration ([Spintwig](https://spintwig.com/short-spx-vertical-put-45-dte-s1-signal-options-backtest/)). Spintwig also found that **45-DTE short SPX calls had negative expected value** ([Spintwig iron condor](https://spintwig.com/short-spx-iron-condor-45-dte-s1-signal-options-backtest/)). That finding argues against iron condors, which add a short-call spread to improve win-rate optics at the cost of expectancy. The Cboe CNDR iron-condor index has been **roughly flat since 2010** ([Options Jive](https://optionsjive.com/blog/iron-condor-options-strategy/); verify against [^CNDR](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/%5ECNDR/)).

Stop-loss rules dominate the results. In one vendor study, the same SPY put-credit-spread configuration returned **+5,400% with a stop-loss and −100% without one**. The same study found realistic fills were 4–7 cents per contract worse than the mid-price, and only 20–25% of posted orders filled ([FlashAlpha, vendor](https://flashalpha.com/articles/spy-put-credit-spread-active-backtest-mm-fills-vrp-signal-drawdown-breaker)). The author withholds the winning parameters, so treat this as evidence of fragility rather than as a rule to copy.

Two Level-3 strategies do not fit the goal. **Covered calls** behave like equity exposure plus a small volatility premium. In AQR's decomposition, the short-volatility component carries only about 10% of the risk ([Israelov & Nielsen](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Papers.cfm?abstract_id=2444999)), so a per-option win rate says little about the result. **The Wheel** failed Spintwig's SPY test. None of the 10 variants beat buy-and-hold, four lost money, and more than 94–99% of each variant's return came from the long stock leg ([Spintwig](https://spintwig.com/spy-wheel-45-dte-options-backtest/)).

### Rule sets to pre-register

The table turns the evidence into exact, backtestable rules. All parameters are fixed in advance, and only the listed grid may be tested.

| ID | Strategy (Level 3) | Exact rules | Grid allowed (log every cell) | Expected win rate | Evidence quality |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P1** | SPY (or SPX) bull put spread | At 15:45 ET, if no position in this expiry: pick the expiry nearest 45 DTE (38–52). Sell the put nearest −0.16 delta and buy the put nearest −0.05 delta (SPX alternative: 25–50 points wide). Record the credit at mid minus 50% of the half-spread. Exit at 50% of credit captured, at 21 DTE, or when the spread mark reaches 2× the credit, whichever comes first. Regime filter: open only if SPY closed above its 200-day SMA. | Short delta {0.16, 0.20, 0.30}; exit {50% / 21 DTE / 2× stop, hold to expiry}; filter {on, off}. **12 cells.** | 85–95% (inference from sources) | Independent (Spintwig, projectfinance) plus vendors; negative since 2010 in the CNDR analog; untested post-2023 |
| **P2** | SPY cash-secured put | Same timing. Sell the put nearest −0.16 delta at about 45 DTE, fully cash-collateralized. Hold to expiry or take profit at 75%. If assigned, sell the shares at the next open (no Wheel). | Delta {0.16, 0.30}; exit {expiry, 75%}. **4 cells.** | ~89–95% (vendor figure) | projectfinance and Spintwig; benchmark against PUT/PUTY and SPY buy-and-hold |
| **C1** | Iron condor (control only) | P1 plus a mirror call spread at +0.16/+0.05 delta. | Same as P1 | ~68% at 16 delta | projectfinance: 71,417 trades, ~68% expired worthless ([source](https://www.projectfinance.com/iron-condor-management/)); call side negative EV |

P1 is the leading candidate. It has defined risk, uses less capital than P2, and Level 3 permits it. Run C1 only to confirm that the call side subtracts value.

## Mean-reversion systems stall at 75–83%, and crypto dies on fees

The equity mean-reversion family is well documented and easy to code, but in published tests it does not reach 85%. On SPY the typical profile is a **75–83% win rate** with the average loss larger than the average win. The Connors RSI(2) system buys when SPY is above its 200-day SMA and RSI(2) falls below 5 (or 10), then exits on a close above the 5-day SMA ([StockCharts](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/trading-strategies-and-models/trading-strategies/rsi-2)). A vendor test reports **75% wins, +0.57% per trade and a 23% maximum drawdown** ([QuantifiedStrategies](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/rsi2-on-spy/)). An independent test from October 2000 found **181 trades with 82% wins**, and the system lost to buy-and-hold after costs ([Guru Finance Insights](https://gurufinanceinsights.substack.com/p/i-backtested-the-classic-rsi2-mean)). Double 7s buys a 7-day closing low above the 200-day SMA and sells a 7-day closing high. On SPY since 1993 it produced **154 trades and 82.5% wins, with an average loser of 2.99% against an average winner of 2.06%** ([QuantifiedStrategies](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/larry-connors-double-seven-strategy-does-it-still-work/)). An IBS-plus-RSI system on the S&P 500 produced **583 trades, 74% wins and a profit factor of 2.73** ([QuantifiedStrategies](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/ibs-internal-bar-strength-indicator-strategies/)). Turn-of-the-month (last 4 plus first 3 trading days) produced **198 trades with 78% wins**, in the market 7% of the time ([QuantifiedStrategies](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/the-turn-of-the-month-effect-with)).

The claims above 90% are marketing. Connors' TPS scale-in system claims **93.98%** accuracy ([TradingStrategyGuides](https://tradingstrategyguides.com/tps-trading-strategy/)), but an apparent independent re-test found about **77% wins with a 0.85 payoff ratio** ([Quantitativo](https://www.quantitativo.com/p/trading-etfs-while-fear-and-greed)). The edge is also fading. ConnorsRSI readings below 5 on stocks were followed by an average **+1.5% next-week return in 1990–2000, but only +0.3% in 2015–2025** ([Concretum Research](https://x.com/ConcretumR/status/1879572346362593308)). Across 72 published strategies, Sharpe ratios fell by about half after publication ([Falck, Rej & Thesmar](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.01380)). Short-term reversal returns work like payment for providing liquidity, and they are strongest when the VIX is high ([Nagel, RFS 2012](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/25/7/2005/1602153)).

Reaching 1,000 trades means running these rules across a stock universe, and that raises a data problem. On one S&P 500 mean-reversion system, current-constituent data showed a **22.48% CAGR versus 14.51%** with survivorship-free data ([hmaquant](https://hmaquant.substack.com/p/survivorship-bias-and-other-data)). Alvarez found the opposite direction for mean reversion, where adding delisted stocks *improved* results ([Alvarez](https://alvarezquanttrading.com/blog/how-much-does-hot-having-survivorship-free-data-changes-test-results/)). Point-in-time constituents are therefore required. A reasonable candidate to pre-register is **M1**: S&P 500 point-in-time members above their 200-day SMA; enter at the next open when IBS < 0.2 and RSI(2) < 10, with VIX above its 1-year median; exit on a close above the 5-day SMA or after 10 days. Treat M1 as a diversifier to evaluate against a lower bar, not as an 85% strategy.

**Crypto should be excluded.** Academic evidence shows the largest, most liquid coins exhibit daily *momentum*, not reversal ([Dobrynskaya et al. 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1057521921002349)). Robinhood's default crypto routing has cost close to **2% round trip on BTC**, and Robinhood receives $0.95 per $100 routed ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/crypto/articles/trading-bitcoin-robinhood-why-2-220000888.html); [Robinhood routing](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/crypto-order-routing/)). A typical 0.5–1% gross dip-buy edge does not survive that cost. Even a vendor reports that BTC RSI(2) with a 200-day MA filter was marginal, with commissions "nearly wiping the edge" ([Coinquant](https://www.coinquant.ai/blog/building-a-mean-reversion-strategy-in-cryptocurrency-markets-evidence-from-78-backtests)).

## Tail losses are the price of every high hit rate

Every candidate is short crash risk, and its losses cluster in the same few episodes. In 2008 the PUT index lost **−26.8%** and BXM lost −28.7%, against −37.0% for the S&P 500 ([Wilshire for Cboe](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/spx/wilshire-options-based-benchmark-indexes-2019.pdf)). Its longest drawdown-and-recovery lasted 30 months ([Bondarenko](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/SSRN_ID3393940_code246693.pdf?abstractid=3393940&mirid=1)). In the February 2018 and February 2020 shocks, the S&P 500 fell more than 10% within two weeks ([Roundhill](https://blog.roundhillinvestments.com/historical-corrections-last-week)). On August 5, 2024, the VIX posted its largest one-day spike on record, and market makers widened out-of-the-money put spreads asymmetrically ([Avantis](https://www.avantisinvestors.com/avantis-insights/lessons-from-market-panic-2024/)). In that environment a stop triggered on an option's mark can fill at an extreme price. For a 16-delta put spread, the maximum loss (width minus credit) is typically **3–6× the credit** (inference). One bad month can therefore erase a year or more of premium.

Mean reversion has its own version of this problem. Connors reported that "the use of stops lowered the performance. Even the 50% stop had lower performance than no stop" ([TradingMarkets](https://tradingmarkets.com/trading-tip/stops-hurt-1597528)). Leaving out stops is part of how the win rate reaches 80%. The 200-day SMA filter keeps the system out of long bear markets, but it cannot protect a position opened just before a crash. Adding a time stop or a catastrophic stop is worth the cost to backtest returns. Position sizing should use a fraction of Kelly (¼ to ½), computed from a *stressed* loss: at least twice the worst historical loss, or the loss in a 1987-, 2008- or 2020-style gap. Sizing from the average loss understates the risk. For P1, that means sizing so that a full-width loss on every open spread at once stays within your drawdown budget.

## A test protocol that cannot be iterated into success

The user explicitly ruled out iterating until the target is hit, and that procedure is exactly what the multiple-testing literature penalizes. With 1,000 variants tried, the best one shows a Sharpe t-statistic of about **3.3 by chance alone** ([Deflated Sharpe Ratio, SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2460551)). With five years of data, no more than about **45 independent configurations** should be tried before an in-sample Sharpe of 1 becomes expected from noise ([Bailey et al., Notices of the AMS](https://www.ams.org/notices/201405/rnoti-p458.pdf)). The protocol below keeps the grid under that limit (P1: 12 cells, P2: 4, C1: 12, M1: at most 12). It commits to a single test on a hold-out and treats a failed hold-out as "burned": you cannot reuse it after changing the rules.

**Data and splits.** OptionsDX offers free end-of-day SPY and SPX chains with bid, ask, IV and greeks for 2010–2023 ([OptionsDX](https://www.optionsdx.com/); [Arias](https://davidariasfinance.com/scripts/free-options-data/)). Use 2010–2019 for development, with rolling walk-forward in which the in-sample window is 4–6× the out-of-sample window ([Pardo via Quanthop](https://quanthop.com/learn/backtesting-optimization/parameter-optimization)). Hold 2020–2023 out and test it once; it contains March 2020 and the 2022 bear market. Then paper-trade or forward-test from 2024 onward on Robinhood fills, which covers August 2024 if you obtain paid or broker historical data. Do not substitute Black-Scholes pricing with the VIX as implied volatility. The VIX ignores the put skew, so it misprices 16-delta and 5-delta puts and misses spread blowouts (inference). Real chain data is also what overturned the earlier model-based results in Kumiega et al. ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7072/12/4/114)). For M1, use point-in-time constituents that include delisted stocks, such as Norgate ([Alvarez review](https://alvarezquanttrading.com/blog/norgate-data-review/)). Use the free Cboe VIX history for the regime filter ([Macroption](https://www.macroption.com/vix-historical-data/)).

**Costs and fills.** Robinhood charges no options commission but collects about **$0.04 per contract** in regulatory and clearing fees, plus a FINRA TAF of **$0.00329 per contract sold** from 2026 ([Robinhood fee schedule](https://cdn.robinhood.com/assets/robinhood/legal/RHF+Fee+Schedule.pdf)). The bid-ask spread is the larger cost. Quoted spreads on liquid equity options average about 8.1 cents per share ([Muravyev & Pearson, RFS 2020](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/33/11/4973/5732665)). Model entries at mid minus 25–50% of the half-spread, and stop-outs at the natural (worst) price. For equities, fill at the next bar's open. When a daily bar could have hit both the target and the stop, assume the stop hit first. Rerun everything at 2× costs.

**Acceptance criteria**, all measured on out-of-sample trades net of costs:

1. **Win rate.** The one-sided 95% Wilson lower bound is at least 0.85, which means about 87% observed over 1,000 trades. Recompute the bound using an effective *n* (distinct non-overlapping trade periods) as well as the raw trade count.
2. **Profitability.** The mean trade has t ≥ 3. The block-bootstrap 95% lower bounds are above 0 for mean return and above 1 for profit factor ([Harvey & Liu](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2345489)).
3. **Selection bias.** The Deflated Sharpe Ratio exceeds 0.95, using the logged number of variants. The probability of backtest overfitting, estimated with combinatorially symmetric cross-validation, is below 0.10 ([Bailey et al., PBO](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2326253)). Both thresholds are practitioner conventions.
4. **Better than random.** A randomized-entry null keeps the same exit rules and holding periods but enters at random. The strategy's expectancy must beat the 95th percentile of the null. This test matters most here, because asymmetric exits alone produce high win rates.
5. **Robustness and tail.** The strategy stays profitable under ±20% parameter changes, in every regime (bull, bear, high VIX), and at 2× costs. The 99th-percentile Monte Carlo drawdown stays within the risk budget, including with a synthetic loss of 10× the average loss inserted.
6. **Benchmark.** P1 and P2 must beat SPY buy-and-hold and the PUT index on a risk-adjusted basis over the same window. Otherwise the strategy is just equity beta dressed up as a high win rate.

## Conclusion

The evidence reverses the premise of the question. An 85% win rate is not rare. It is the default shape of any strategy that sells insurance, and it says almost nothing about whether money is being made. The binding constraint is net expectancy over an effective sample large enough to include a crash. Independent work since 2010 suggests that premium sellers now earn little beyond equity beta, so the most likely honest outcome of the protocol above is a strategy that clears 85% wins but fails the benchmark or t ≥ 3 test. That result would still be useful. It tells you P1 is worth running only as a lower-volatility substitute for owning the index, sized for its worst month rather than its average one.

The candidate with a real chance of passing is P1 with its regime filter. It is also the one most sensitive to its stop rule and to fill quality during stress, and those are the two things a free end-of-day backtest models worst. That is why forward testing on live Robinhood quotes through at least one volatility shock is a required part of the test rather than an optional extra. A strategy whose edge disappears once its fills are real never had an edge.

## What the evidence says about getting in early
- Ritter (1991, data updated to 2025): IPOs average a ~16% first-day pop, then lag matched firms
  by ~27 points over 3 years. Firms that list in hot, high-volume years do worst. 2026 is a hot
  AI listing year.
- Ben-David et al. (NBER w28369): theme ETFs launch after their theme's big run, then deliver
  about -6%/yr alpha over the next five years.

## Our tests (Yahoo daily data, adjusted closes, vs QQQ over the same days)
1. **Buy at the first close, hold 1 year** (58 US IPOs/listings 2019-2025, tech-heavy; list
   from memory, so survivorship bias flatters it):
   - median -19% vs QQQ +13%;
   - beat QQQ only 33% of the time; one in three lost more than half;
   - the mean (+21%) comes from a few huge winners (PLTR, NBIS, ARM, UPST's first year).
   It is a lottery ticket: the typical outcome is a loss.
2. **Wait for proof:** buy only when the stock closes above its first-20-day high, exit when it
   closes below its 50-day average (max 1 year). 44 trades:
   - worst trade -23% instead of -90%+;
   - median -6% (-5% vs QQQ), mean +5.6% vs QQQ (t 1.2), 34% win, ~31-day holds.
   Better risk, but no reliable edge. Not adoptable (t < 2, small sample); added to the backlog
   to retest on a bigger, unbiased IPO list.
3. **AI-named stocks since listing:** big winners (PLTR, ARM, ALAB, NBIS, RDDT) and wipeouts
   (C3.ai -88%, UiPath -81%, BigBear -73%, SoundHound -20%). Every one fell at least 54% from a
   peak at some point.
4. **AI/robotics theme ETFs since launch:** ROBO +9.6%/yr and BOTZ +9.5%/yr vs QQQ ~+19-20%/yr;
   AIQ matched QQQ. Only the 2023 launch CHAT beat it, riding the current boom.
5. **AI "picks and shovels" = semiconductors (SMH, since 2000):**
   - 2001-2014: +1.2%/yr buy & hold (DD -75%), -0.2%/yr even with the 200-day trend rule (DD -54%).
   - 2015-2026: +31.5%/yr.
   - Adding SMH to the SPY/QQQ rotation: 2015+ +21%/yr but 2001-2014 only +4.7%/yr with DD -30%.
   It fails the in-sample test: a pure bet that the current regime continues. The last tech
   boom (2000) looked the same from the inside.

## Decision
- **No IPO-day or "AI theme" buys with real money.** None of these passed the adoption test,
  and the typical early buyer loses.
- **The account already owns the AI leaders** through the core: the rotation half currently
  holds QQQ, whose top holdings are NVDA, MSFT, AAPL, AVGO, AMZN, META and GOOGL. The trend and
  6-month-momentum exits are what protect that exposure in a bust like 2000-2002 or 2022.
- **For new AI listings (Anthropic/OpenAI if they list):** no buy in the first 20 trading days.
  After that, only via a rule that has passed the adoption test (the breakout + 50-day rule is
  the current candidate; it needs a larger unbiased test first). Each name also needs its own
  research note (valuation vs revenue, lockup date, float) before any trade.
- Robinhood IPO Access allocations at the offer price are a different trade, because they avoid
  the day-one pop. They are usually tiny or zero for small accounts, and the 3-year evidence
  above still applies. Not pursued.

# Signal-Driven Buying of Options (Long Calls/Puts, Debit Spreads): Empirical Evidence

Scope: US equity and index options. Research date: late September 2026. Several primary PDFs (SSRN, Cboe CDN, LSU, Purdue, ResearchGate, Substack) could not be fetched from this environment (egress blocked), so some numbers come from search-result abstracts or snippets and not from full-text reads. Those are flagged "(abstract/snippet)".

## 1. Structural headwinds: volatility risk premium, theta, IV crush, spreads, lottery overpricing

### Takeaway
Buying options unconditionally has strongly negative expected returns, and this is one of the best-documented results in empirical finance. Index straddles lose about 3% per week, ATM index puts lose about 30% per month, deep OTM index puts lose about 95% per month, and 30-day variance swaps lose about 66% on average. Lottery-like OTM calls on single stocks are further overpriced by 10-20% per month relative to comparable calls. Any signal has to overcome this drag before it earns anything.

### Cited Findings
- **Coval & Shumway (2001, JF, "Expected Option Returns")**: zero-beta ATM S&P 500 (SPX) straddles return about **-3% per week** on average, and ATM SPX straddles specifically return **-3.15% per week**. Zero-beta straddle returns are negative at every strike tested. Sample: SPX/OEX options, roughly the late 1980s to mid-1990s (abstract/snippet) — [SSRN](https://www.ssrn.com/abstract=239435); [paper PDF](https://business.baylor.edu/don_cunningham/Option%20Returns.pdf)
- **Bakshi & Kapadia (2003, RFS)**: delta-hedged long S&P 500 index option portfolios **underperform zero**. The underperformance is smaller for options away from the money and **larger when volatility is high**, and it persists after controlling for jump fears. The authors read this as a negative market volatility risk premium, meaning option buyers pay for volatility insurance (abstract/snippet) — [PDF](https://people.umass.edu/~nkapadia/docs/Bakshi_and_Kapadia_2003_RFS.pdf); [OUP](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/16/2/527/1579962)
- **Carr & Wu (2009, RFS, "Variance Risk Premiums")**: the average variance risk premium is strongly negative for the S&P 500, S&P 100 and DJIA. Going long a 30-day S&P 500 variance swap and holding it to maturity produced an average excess return of **-66%** over their sample, which ran from about 1996 to 2003 (snippet) — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Variance-Risk-Premiums-Carr-Wu/ee61789ce93bff6274e17f46dbbd797b76a436e6)
- **Broadie, Chernov & Johannes (2009, RFS, "Understanding Index Option Returns")**: average monthly S&P 500 put returns are about **-30% for ATM puts**, about **-60% for puts 6% OTM** and about **-95% for deep OTM puts**. The authors argue these losses are largely consistent with standard option-pricing models, because buyers are paying for leverage and crash exposure. They are not necessarily evidence of mispricing (snippet) — [Columbia PDF](http://www.columbia.edu/~mnb2/broadie/Assets/EOR_20080618.pdf); [OUP](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/22/11/4493/1568222)
- **Boyer & Vorkink (2014, "Stock Options as Lotteries", JF)**: calls on the **most lottery-like stocks underperform** similar calls on the least lottery-like stocks by **10-20% per month**. Ex-ante skewness, measured from moneyness and underlying volatility, is negatively related to option returns (snippet) — [BYU](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/8927/); [Internet appendix](https://boyer.byu.edu/00000180-910c-dc15-abb6-d9dfb7040001/boyer-vorkink-appendix-pdf)
- A related paper, "Gambling preference and individual equity option returns" (JFE), finds that demand for lottery-like calls pushes their prices above the level implied by the underlying, with more frequent put-call parity violations in the direction of overpriced calls (snippet) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X1630109X)
- **Muravyev & Pearson (2020, RFS, "Options Trading Costs Are Lower than You Think")**: conventional effective spreads overstate costs for traders who time their executions. Across all trades, effective spreads are about **one-quarter to one-third smaller** than conventional estimates. For "timers", who buy when fair value is near the ask, they are **under 40% of conventional measures**, which the authors also describe as about 5x smaller. The flip side is that a non-timing retail market-order buyer still pays roughly the conventional spread (abstract/snippet) — [OUP](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/33/11/4973/5732665); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2580548); [CXO summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-options/timing-option-trades-to-suppress-trading-frictions/)
- **Beckmeyer, Branger & Gayda (2023, "Retail Traders Love 0DTE Options... But Should They?")**: retail **effective spreads on SPX 0DTE were 6.1% of premium for calls and 4.9% for puts**, against 13.1% and 10.1% for non-retail trades. Even so, about **60% of retail daily losses came from transaction costs** (snippet/press) — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4404704); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-04-21/day-traders-lose-358-000-per-day-gambling-on-zero-day-options)

### Inferences
- The volatility risk premium is largest in **index** options and in **high-volatility regimes** (Bakshi & Kapadia). Buying index puts or straddles after volatility has already spiked is therefore the costliest time in VRP terms, though the directional bet can still pay.
- Buying cheap OTM options stacks three costs: the VRP, a lottery or skew premium (Boyer & Vorkink; Broadie et al.) and the widest percentage spreads. A small account that buys $0.20-$1.00 options is structurally in the most expensive corner of the market.
- A round-trip spread cost of 5-10% of premium per trade (implied by the 0DTE figures) means a signal needs a gross edge well above 10% per trade on premium just to break even.

### Gaps
- I could not open the full text of Goyal & Saretto or Coval & Shumway to confirm exact sample periods and cost tables.
- I found no clean published estimate of per-trade theta or IV-crush drag specifically for signal-timed short-dated single-stock calls.

## 2. When have long options worked? (IV-RV cheapness, term structure, momentum, dip-buy signals, VIX spikes, tail hedges)

### Takeaway
The strongest academic evidence of positive returns from **buying** options is cross-sectional and volatility-based. Goyal & Saretto (2009) and Vasquez (2017) show that straddles on stocks whose implied volatility is low relative to historical volatility, or whose IV term structure slopes steeply upward, earn high returns. These are long-short, delta-neutral, monthly portfolio results before costs, and they are not directional call buying. Directional dip-buy signals such as SPY RSI(2) and RSI plus VIX filters have high win rates on **shares**, but I found no credible published backtest showing that the same signal is profitable when expressed through long calls after costs. Perpetual put protection is a documented long-run loser.

### Cited Findings
**Volatility-based (cross-sectional) option buying**
- **Goyal & Saretto (2009, JFE, "Cross-section of option returns and volatility")**: they sort stocks on historical realized volatility minus ATM implied volatility. A long-short decile portfolio of **straddles**, long where RV is well above IV (options look cheap) and short where IV is well above RV, earns **22.7% per month with a Sharpe ratio of 0.710**. Delta-hedged calls and puts show similar patterns. Sample: US equity options, OptionMetrics, about 1996-2006 (abstract/snippet) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X09001251); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v94y2009i2p310-326.html)
- **Vasquez (2017, "Equity Volatility Term Structures and the Cross-Section of Option Returns", JFQA)**: straddles on stocks with the **steepest upward IV term structure**, measured as long-term IV minus 1-month IV, outperform those with the flattest or inverted slope. The hedge portfolio earns **27.1% per month gross, with 14.8% from the long side and 12.2% from the short side** (snippet) — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1944298); [EFMA PDF](https://efmaefm.org/0efmameetings/efma%20annual%20meetings/2015-Amsterdam/papers/EFMA2015_0530_fullpaper.pdf); [CXO summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/volatility-effects/exploiting-the-implied-volatility-term-structure/)
- A later literature comparison lists the Goyal-Saretto volatility differential and the Vasquez IV slope as the two strongest predictors of option returns in its sample (snippet) — [ResearchGate listing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/222532340_Cross-section_of_option_returns_and_volatility)
- **Heston, Jones, Khorram, Li & Mo (2023, JF, "Option Momentum")**: option returns, mainly straddle returns, show **momentum over 6-36 month formation periods**, with long-short **annualized Sharpe ratios above 1.5**. They reverse over short horizons (snippet) — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.13279); [PDF](http://faculty.marshall.usc.edu/Christopher-Jones/pdf/opmom.pdf)

**Directional dip-buy signals (shares, not options)**
- **Connors-style RSI(2) on SPY** (practitioner Substack): buy when RSI(2) is below 10 with a trend filter; exit when RSI(2) is above 70 or after 10 days. Over 26 years this gave **181 trades, an 82% win rate, a 3.7-day average hold, a -13.8% max drawdown** against -55.2% for buy-and-hold, and a Sharpe of 0.62 against 0.53. Compounded growth was lower than buy-and-hold ($2.49 vs $8.68 per $1). These results are for SPY shares, not options — [Substack](https://gurufinanceinsights.substack.com/p/i-backtested-the-classic-rsi2-mean)
- A second, less verifiable practitioner source reports SPY RSI(2) from 2000 to 2024: **387 trades, 73% win rate, +1.8% average win, -2.1% average loss, profit factor 1.65**, 9.4% annualized return while invested 22% of the time. This is also on shares — [backtesteverything](https://www.backtesteverything.com/blog/rsi-mean-reversion-strategy-complete-backtest)
- An **RSI below 30 plus VIX above 20** trigger on SPX (practitioner Substack; I could not open the full page): **57 triggers, 85.96% win rate**, average winner +2.08% over about 3.7 days, average loser -2.20% over about 9.9 days, profit factor 5.64. This is on the index, not options — [Substack](https://backtest.substack.com/p/when-spy-pulls-back-and-vix-spikes)
- Typical oversold-bounce winners are about +1.8-2.1% on the underlying over about 3-4 days. That is small relative to the premium of a 1-2 week ATM SPY call.

**Cboe customer-trade evidence (vendor-published; flag bias)**
- **Cboe-hosted paper, "New Evidence on the Performance of Customer Options Trades"**: Cboe customer SLIM trades are on average profitable. **Purchases of calls** and sales of puts performed positively in both sample periods, while purchases of puts and sales of calls did not. Aggregate held-to-expiration performance improved from **-$1.75M per day to +$0.36M per day** in a Jan 2020-Jun 2023 sample. Cboe benefits commercially from retail options volume, so treat this as possibly biased. The call-buying profit also coincides with a strong bull market (snippet) — [Cboe PDF](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/education/research_publications/Retail_Profitability.pdf)

**Tail hedging and put buying**
- **Israelov (2017, J. Alternative Investments, "Pathetic Protection", AQR)**: put protection reduces drawdowns only if options carry no VRP and drawdowns line up with expiration cycles. With realistic VRP, protected portfolios can show **worse** drawdowns per unit of return than simply holding less equity. The study uses simulated put protection and the Cboe S&P 500 5% Put Protection Index — [AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/White-Papers/Pathetic-Protection-The-Elusive-Benefits-of-Protective-Puts); [CFA digest](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2019/06/dig-v49-n6-2)
- **AQR, "Chasing Your Own Tail (Risk)" and "Revisited"**: a 5% OTM put-protected US 60/40 portfolio returned **7.0% against 9.0%** unprotected. Index put buying has about a **-1.1 long-run Sharpe ratio before trading costs** (snippet). AQR sells alternative approaches such as trend following, so there is some commercial bias — [AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/White-Papers/Chasing-Your-Own-Tail-Risk-Revisited); [PDF](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/White-Papers/AQR-Chasing-Your-Own-Tail-Risk.pdf)
- For a counter-view defending tail hedges (Spitznagel/Universa style), see the Leptokurtic blog post. It is an opinion piece — [link](https://federicocarrone.com/series/leptokurtic/the-tail-hedge-debate-spitznagel-is-right/)

### Inferences
- The positive "buy options" results in the literature come from **relative value in volatility**: buying options that are cheap relative to realized volatility or the term structure, usually hedged against a short leg. They are not about predicting direction. A single long straddle on the cheap decile keeps the long leg's return (about 14.8% per month gross in Vasquez), but that figure is before costs and comes from diversified monthly portfolios of many stocks. One small account cannot replicate it.
- High-win-rate dip-buy signals on shares win small (+2%) and lose small (-2%). With calls, theta over a 3-10 day hold plus the round-trip spread consumes a large part of a +2% underlying move. Losers held about 10 days, as in the VIX-filter test, bleed extra theta. In principle, deep ITM (high delta) calls or call debit spreads keep more of the edge than OTM calls. I found no verified published numbers on this.
- Buying calls after VIX spikes means paying elevated IV (the highest-VRP regime, per Bakshi & Kapadia). A vol mean reversion (IV crush) as the market bounces can offset part of the directional gain. Call debit spreads partly neutralize this.

### Gaps
- **I found no credible peer-reviewed or well-documented backtest** of SPY RSI(2), or other oversold signals, **expressed with long calls or call debit spreads**, with realistic bid-ask costs. Most online claims are from vendor blogs.
- I could not verify Goyal & Saretto's or Vasquez's **net-of-transaction-cost** results in full text. My recollection is that profitability shrinks substantially when trading at full quoted spreads, but I did not confirm this, so it is not asserted here.
- I found no quantified study of buying SPX or SPY calls specifically at VIX spike or capitulation points with option-level P&L.

## 3. Debit spreads vs outright long options: do spreads reduce the VRP drag?

### Takeaway
There is little direct empirical evidence comparing signal-timed debit spreads with outright long options. Theory and the moneyness evidence suggest that selling the further-OTM leg recovers some of the most overpriced (lottery or skew) premium and cuts vega and IV-crush exposure. The cost is capped upside and two spreads paid instead of one.

### Cited Findings
- Returns become sharply more negative the further OTM the option: index puts go from about -30% per month ATM to about -60% at 6% OTM and about -95% deep OTM (Broadie, Chernov & Johannes). For single stocks, the most lottery-like calls underperform by 10-20% per month (Boyer & Vorkink). This implies the short OTM leg of a debit spread is, on average, a sale of the more overpriced option — [Columbia PDF](http://www.columbia.edu/~mnb2/broadie/Assets/EOR_20080618.pdf); [BYU](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/8927/)
- Bakshi & Kapadia find delta-hedged underperformance is **smaller for options away from the money** in the index market. So the VRP per unit of vega is not uniform, and a spread's net VRP exposure depends on strikes (snippet) — [PDF](https://people.umass.edu/~nkapadia/docs/Bakshi_and_Kapadia_2003_RFS.pdf)
- **Vilkov et al., 0DTE strategies paper (SPXW, 09/2016-01/2026)**, open-source repository: unconditional **directional spreads have highly variable outcomes, with interquartile ranges often symmetric around zero**. After half-spread execution and 0.5bp slippage, many structures that were positive gross turn **negative net**. Under out-of-sample conditional direction signals (April 2019 onward), **put ratio spreads** reached a gross Sharpe of 1.18 and a net Sharpe of 0.93, and diversified baskets a net Sharpe of about 0.82. These are working-paper results — [GitHub annotated paper](https://github.com/vilkovgr/0dte-strategies/blob/main/docs/paper/paper-annotated.md)
- Tastytrade and other broker material describes long call verticals as having risk limited to the debit and capped profit. I found no published comparative win-rate or EV study for the two structures — [tastytrade](https://tastytrade.com/learn/trading-products/options/long-call-vertical-spread/)

### Inferences
- A debit spread is roughly a directional bet with reduced net vega and theta, so it is less exposed to the VRP and IV crush than an outright long option. It remains a leveraged directional bet and does not create an edge by itself; the edge still has to come from the signal.
- On small, cheap spreads, paying two bid-ask spreads can take a large share of the debit. Using limit orders near mid matters, per Muravyev & Pearson's finding that timed executions cost far less than conventional spreads.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed study of "signal plus call debit spread vs signal plus long call" on US equities or indices with costs.

## 4. 0DTE options: retail profitability evidence (2023-2026)

### Takeaway
Retail 0DTE activity loses money in aggregate. Losses are concentrated in **buying** 0DTE options, especially puts, and are driven largely by spreads. The one major dissent is the trader-level study by Bogousslavsky & Muravyev, which finds per-trade losses are small, plus the Cboe-published study. Sellers of 0DTE variance earn a small, statistically positive VRP with severe tails.

### Cited Findings
- **Beckmeyer, Branger & Gayda (Univ. of Münster, 2023)**: retail traders lost an average of **$358,000 per day** on SPX 0DTE since daily expirations launched (May 2022). Over the full sample the average was $184,000 per day, and the total was **over $70M** across about 2 years. About **60% of losses came from transaction costs** and about **60% from 0DTE puts**, and **retail buys performed particularly poorly**. Retail effective spreads were 6.1% for calls and 4.9% for puts (press/snippet) — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4404704); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-04-21/day-traders-lose-358-000-per-day-gambling-on-zero-day-options); [Portfolio123 thread](https://community.portfolio123.com/t/day-traders-lose-358-000-per-day-gambling-on-zero-day-odte-options/75326)
- **Bogousslavsky & Muravyev ("An Anatomy of Retail Option Trading", 2024-2025)**, a trader-level study of about **$15-20B of retail trades, 2020-2022**: option trades are **dominated by short-term purchases**, concentrated in SPX and 0DTE, with a **median holding period of about half an hour** (mean 3.5 days). Losses are **relatively small despite wide spreads**. The paper contrasts this with other studies reporting **3-9% losses per trade** using aggregate retail proxies (snippet) — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4682388); [Illinois Experts](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/an-anatomy-of-retail-option-trading/)
- One search summary gave the figure "0DTE option trades lose 4.7% relative to other option trades, while non-0DTE trades earn 0.19%". It appears to come from the retail-trading literature above, but I could not confirm which paper, so treat the attribution as unverified — [Cboe/LSU search context](https://www.lsu.edu/business/files/event-files/2025-finance-mardi-gras/retail_option_trading_v2.pdf)
- A Medium blog (secondary; low reliability) reports retail **debit** 0DTE trades lose about **-$8.05 per contract**, while **credit** trades make about **+$4.55 per contract** — [Medium](https://medium.com/@submartingaleoptions/retail-traders-lose-350k-daily-on-zero-days-to-expiry-0dte-options-please-read-this-if-you-25d46bdd1bfb)
- **Vilkov et al. 0DTE paper (09/2016-01/2026)**: 0DTE options carry a **positive, statistically significant but economically small VRP**. The median realized VRP from 10:00 ET to expiry is about 0.0011% of the underlying. **ATM-to-OTM calls and most puts lose money on average for buyers**. Short slightly-OTM options profit in up to 75% of observations, with 1% expected shortfall of 0.58-1.58% of the underlying. The authors' conclusion: "Unconditional 0DTE exposure is difficult to justify as a standing allocation" — [GitHub](https://github.com/vilkovgr/0dte-strategies/blob/main/docs/paper/paper-annotated.md)
- **Cboe-hosted study** (vendor bias): aggregate customer performance was positive, at +$0.36M per day for Jan 2020-Jun 2023, and call purchases were profitable. This contradicts the Münster findings on sign — [Cboe PDF](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/education/research_publications/Retail_Profitability.pdf)
- Option Alpha, a vendor with user-platform data, reports that iron butterflies and iron condors made up 78% of 0DTE positions on its platform, with win rates of 66.76% and 70.19%. These are short-premium structures, not long options — [Option Alpha](https://optionalpha.com/blog/0dte-options-strategy-performance)

### Inferences
- The balance of independent evidence says retail **buying** of 0DTE options has negative expected value, mainly because of spreads, which are 5-6% of premium even for retail, plus a small VRP. The findings conflict on magnitude: large aggregate losses in Münster versus small per-trade losses in Bogousslavsky & Muravyev. Cboe-published work that shows profits should be discounted for vendor bias and sample period (a strong 2020-2023 equity market).

### Gaps
- I found no 2025-2026 peer-reviewed update specifically measuring retail 0DTE P&L after the 2024-2025 growth of single-stock and ETF daily expirations.
- I could not open the full Bogousslavsky & Muravyev or Cboe papers, so their exact dollar and percentage figures by DTE are unverified.
